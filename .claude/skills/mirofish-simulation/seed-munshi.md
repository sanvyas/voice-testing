# Seed brief: Munshi launches among small business owners in Pune (for a MiroFish run)

This is background for a simulation. It states facts and the setting only. It deliberately contains no predicted adoption numbers and no results from earlier simulated panels, so the simulation is an independent signal.

## The product (facts as of November 2026)
Munshi is an AI call assistant for small businesses in India, built by a two-person team. When the owner does not pick up for about 20 seconds, Munshi answers in Hindi, Hinglish or English, says at once that it is an AI assistant, finds out the caller's name and what they need, and takes the order or message. The owner sees a one-line summary per call in a simple app, sorted into "Needs you", "Good to know" and "Not worth it" (sales and loan calls). The caller also gets a WhatsApp message with the shop's address and hours. Munshi never quotes a price it has not been given and never promises a discount, delivery time or stock. The owner keeps a price board by photographing a rate list or saying "toor dal 170 ho gayi"; a rate more than two days old is not quoted. People the owner marks "always ring me" (family, regulars, suppliers) are never answered by Munshi. Sign-in is by phone number. Price: Rs 499 a month for the standard plan (up to 100 answered calls), a Rs 299 Starter, a Rs 999 Pro. 14 days free, no card. The team sets it up for the owner in one WhatsApp chat; the owner dials one call-forwarding code on their own phone.

## Honest status
The owner app, call handling, price board and summaries work in testing. The phone line (a dedicated number and call forwarding) and WhatsApp sending are not live yet, because they wait on telecom registration and Meta business verification. No paying customers exist. The Instagram account has just started and has a few posts and reels in Hindi and Hinglish. The AI speaks with a text-to-speech voice that is clearly synthetic.

## Alternatives an owner knows or can find
- Doing nothing: the owner picks up when they can; missed calls go to the next shop.
- Family or staff answering (a nephew, a spouse, a hired receptionist at Rs 15,000 to 25,000 a month).
- Missed-call number services that send an automatic SMS or WhatsApp reply (Edesy at Rs 2,999 a month, WappBlaster about Rs 2,499 a year, MyOperator from Rs 5,000 a month).
- AI receptionists aimed at firms and clinics (Edesy Rs 50,000 set-up plus Rs 5,000 a month; CallMissed from about USD 29 a month).
- Plain call forwarding to a second phone.

## The world to simulate: the owner community in Pune
A community of about thirty people who run or work with small businesses in Pune and nearby, active in WhatsApp groups and on Instagram, with the platform standing in for a trade-association group.
- Kirana and general store owners (Kothrud, Hadapsar, Pimpri), a mix of ages 28 to 58, some with a nephew at the counter, some alone.
- A wholesale dal and rice trader in Market Yard who quotes rates by phone all day.
- A sweet shop owner whose regular customers phone in festival orders.
- A salon owner and a boutique tailor who are with a customer when the phone rings.
- A small garment or packaging manufacturer with a noisy factory floor and quotations by phone.
- A clinic receptionist and a coaching class owner.
- A garage owner who is under cars all day.
- Two early adopters who already use WhatsApp Business, UPI tools and Instagram.
- A sceptical senior member of the local traders' association who was burned by an IVR system and an app before, and whose opinion carries weight in the WhatsApp group.
- A local CA (accountant) who many owners ask about software and subscriptions.
- A telecom shop owner who knows how call forwarding and its charges work on Airtel, Jio, Vi and BSNL.
- A Hindi tech and small-business creator on Instagram with a following among owners.
- The Munshi founder, answering questions honestly, and one Munshi support person.
- A competitor's salesperson who contacts owners with a more expensive missed-call service.

## The event
Munshi opens a 14-day free trial to owners in this community through Instagram reels, a post in the traders' WhatsApp group by the founder, and a few personal demos. The simulation runs for the first 30 days. Owners discuss it with each other, ask the founder questions, try it, compare it with the alternatives above, and decide whether to keep it.
